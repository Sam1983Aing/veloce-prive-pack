How every prompt is built

The same seven parts for every car. Only the car, the place and the shots change.

  1. ReferenceOne still of the car, taken from the 3D page. Name the car in capitals (THE RED SPIDER) and describe what must not change: paint, wheels, details.
  2. SceneWhere and when, in one line: a cliff road at golden hour, Monaco at sunset.
  3. StyleHow it should feel: relentless speed, heavy speed blur, very low angles, a camera that chases the car.
  4. Shot 1, 2, 3Three timed shots that add up to 15 s, each with a camera position and one action. Say how each cut happens (a hard cut on the rev, a whip-pan, a white flare).
  5. SoundThe engine, the road and the score, and how the score changes at the end.
  6. EndingTell it how to finish: slow down, blur, fade to a fully black screen. Without this the car just drives out of frame.
  7. LocksWhat must stay true: the exact car, an anonymous driver, real weight, no added text.

The trailer

Three drafts, one prompt layout

Cinema Studio 4.0 · 30 s · all the cars in one film · 3 drafts at 480p, the final one upscaled to 1080p

How a trailer prompt is laid out

The same five parts for all three drafts. Only the story and the shots change.

  1. Scene contextWhat the film is about and how it should feel, so every shot belongs to one story.
  2. Active referencesEach car named once, with a short name the shots use after that. It's a cast list.
  3. Timed shotsEleven to thirteen numbered shots that add up to 30 s, each with one job and one named camera setup. No car gets the same kind of shot as another.
  4. AudioWhat's heard over black, how the score builds, where it goes silent.
  5. Positive locksWhat must stay the same in every shot: the exact cars, anonymous drivers, real weight, no text.
